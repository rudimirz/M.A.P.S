Sources & credits

Ganymede mixes an invented rescue story with real space science. Every number in the game carries a tag, so you always know which is which.

REALMeasured or published by NASA, JPL or ESA: planet positions, sunlight at Jupiter, cosmic-ray dose, engine performance, the speed of light.
ESTIMATEOur rounded or scaled number where no exact source fits: part masses, data rates, and travel times for a crewed nuclear ship.
FICTIONStory only: the ocean plague, the Gany-Phyta algae, gold in the ice, the Áurea consortium and the Lifter rockets.

NASA data and resources

  • JPL Solar System Dynamics · Approximate Positions of the Planets ↗Keplerian elements for Earth, Mars and Jupiter (Table 1, valid 1800–2050). The game computes where each planet is for every launch month from 2031 to 2034. That drives the launch window, the orbit map and the signal delay.
  • NASA NSSDC · Jupiter Fact Sheet ↗Sunlight at Jupiter of 50.26 W/m² (Earth 1,361) and Jupiter’s 4,332.6-day year, or 30.35° per Earth year. Used in the power budget and the launch window.
  • NASA Technical Reports Server · nuclear thermal rockets ↗Specific impulse of about 900 s, twice the best chemical engines. Used in the rocket equation for route fuel.
  • NASA/JPL · Juno breaks the solar-power distance record ↗Juno’s arrays make about 14 kW at Earth but only about 500 W at Jupiter. Used to explain why our ship runs on fission reactors.
  • NASA · Juno mission and trajectory ↗Launch energy C3 ≈ 31 km²/s², deep-space burns in 2012, Earth flyby in 2013 and a 0.54 km/s braking burn at Jupiter in 2016. Our Earth-flyby route and the Jupiter braking maths follow Juno.
  • NASA · Life Support Baseline Values and Assumptions ↗Food, water and oxygen per crew member per day. We use about 2.3 kg per person per day with recycling to size the supplies for each route.
  • NASA · Curiosity’s Radiation Assessment Detector ↗About 1.8 mSv per day measured on the cruise to Mars. Used for the crew’s cruise dose.
  • NASA-STD-3001 · crew health standard ↗Career radiation limit of 600 mSv. Used to judge the crew’s dose.
  • NASA Deep Space Network ↗Three dish sites shared by every deep-space mission. Used in the data downlink calculation.
  • NASA Image and Video Library ↗Six photos of Europa Clipper’s Falcon Heavy launch (KSC, 14 Oct 2024) for the launch sequence, and PIA26435, Europa Clipper’s trajectory. Title image PIA22946: NASA/JPL-Caltech/SwRI/MSSS/Kevin M. Gill.
  • NASA mission pages · Europa Clipper, Juno, Galileo, Cassini, Artemis ↗Real missions linked in each level report to the choices you just made.
  • NASA NSSDC · Jovian Satellite Fact Sheet ↗Ganymede’s GM (9,887.8 km³/s²) and radius (2,631 km). Used for the Level 3 landing burn.
  • NASA/JPL · Galileo temperature map of Ganymede ↗Surface temperatures of 70–152 K. Used for each landing site, the habitat heat loss and the melt-drill maths.
  • NASA · Ganymede images for Level 3 ↗PIA00600 (Galileo, Jupiter and its moons), PIA02862 (Cassini, Ganymede and Jupiter), PIA25012 (Juno), PIA00276 and PIA00278 (Galileo terrain), PIA03781 (global map), PIA18005 (interior, artist’s concept).

Why mass matters

A rocket pushes itself by burning fuel, and that fuel has mass too. Every extra tonne of cargo needs more fuel, which needs more fuel to lift it. That is the rocket equation, and it is why the same rocket can carry much less to Jupiter than to Earth orbit.

Why water stops radiation

A solar storm throws out protons at a large fraction of the speed of light. Water is full of hydrogen, whose nucleus is a single proton. Hitting something of the same mass slows a proton far more than hitting a heavy metal atom, like one billiard ball stopping another. That is why crews shelter behind water during solar storms.
